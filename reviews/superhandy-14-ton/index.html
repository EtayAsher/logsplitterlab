Verdict

The highest-capacity electric splitter in our catalog — 14 tons and a 16 in. log diameter rating, meaningfully above every 6.5–7-ton electric model we cover, for homeowners who want more force without moving to gas.

Best for

Homeowners who want noticeably more splitting force and larger log capacity than a 6.5-ton electric unit, but still want to avoid fuel, exhaust, and engine maintenance.

Key limitation

Anyone who wants the lightest, most compact electric option — at 126 lb it's the heaviest electric model in our catalog — or who needs a confirmed warranty term before buying, which we could not verify for this specific model.

Key strengths

  • 14-ton rating and 16 in. log diameter capacity — the largest of any electric model in our catalog, matching the log-diameter rating of our SuperHandy GUO077 gas model despite running on household power
  • 16-second cycle time and a 10 GPM 2-stage Bucher hydraulic pump, per the manufacturer and cross-referenced retailer data
  • No fuel, exhaust, or engine maintenance despite the higher tonnage

Key limitations

  • Heaviest electric model in our catalog (126 lb) — less convenient for one person to reposition than the lighter 6.5–7-ton electrics
  • Warranty term could not be confirmed for this specific model
  • Still tied to a power outlet and an appropriately rated extension cord, and a higher-wattage motor draws more current than the compact electrics in our catalog

Power and expected workload

Suited to homeowners who split more wood, or larger-diameter rounds, than a 6.5-ton electric splitter comfortably handles, without wanting to move to a gas engine.

Cycle time

A 16-second cycle time, per the manufacturer, is within the range of the other electric models in our catalog (18–19s for the 6.5-ton models) despite the higher tonnage — a real, verified advantage of its 2-stage hydraulic pump design.

Log size considerations

Rated for logs up to 16 in. diameter and 20 in. length, per the manufacturer — matching our SuperHandy GUO077 gas model's diameter rating exactly, and well above every other electric model in our catalog (9.8–10 in.).

Portability

Ships with an integrated handle and wheels for repositioning around a garage or yard, per cross-referenced retailer data; it is not designed for towing behind a vehicle.

Setup and storage

Assembly is required on first setup. Store indoors or under cover; there's no fuel to manage before storage, but check hydraulic fluid periodically — the 2.1-gallon tank (AW32 or AW46 fluid, per cross-referenced retailer data) is larger than the compact 6.5-ton electrics in our catalog.

Noise and electrical requirements

Runs on a standard 120V/15A circuit at 1800W, per the manufacturer — a higher draw than the 6.5-ton electrics in our catalog, so confirm your circuit isn't shared with other high-draw tools. Quieter than a gas engine, with no exhaust.

Usability

A 10 GPM, 2-stage Bucher hydraulic gear pump (per cross-referenced retailer data) and a 16 in. cylinder stroke drive the 14-ton rating — notably, our SuperHandy GUO077 gas model also uses a Bucher-brand pump, though we haven't confirmed the two share a manufacturer the way Landworks GUO079 and GUO077 do.

Maintenance expectations

Check the hydraulic fluid level (2.1-gallon / 8L capacity, AW32 or AW46 recommended, per cross-referenced retailer data) and inspect the power cord and plug before each use. Fluid types and service intervals vary by model — always follow the manufacturer's manual rather than a generic schedule. See our maintenance guide for general upkeep concepts.

Safety notes

Operate this splitter only as described in the manufacturer's manual. Keep hands clear of the wedge and ram path, split one log at a time unless the machine is explicitly rated otherwise, and wear eye protection and sturdy footwear. See our general safety guidance — it does not replace the manufacturer's instructions for this specific model.

Who should buy it

Choose this if you want meaningfully more splitting force and log capacity than a 6.5-ton electric model, and you're willing to trade some portability (it's the heaviest electric model we cover) for that capability.

FAQ

Is the SuperHandy GUO084 the same as the SuperHandy GUO077 in your catalog?

No — they're genuinely different products. GUO077 is a 20-ton gas splitter; GUO084 is a 14-ton electric splitter. Different power source, different tonnage, different model number. The only thing they share, per our research, is a Bucher-brand hydraulic pump.

Is 14 tons overkill for an electric splitter?

Not necessarily — it fills a real gap between the compact 6.5–7-ton electric models and a full gas splitter, for homeowners who want more force than a small electric unit but still want to avoid fuel and engine maintenance.
